Replacing the old battery on rechargeable bike lights

Published September 27, 2026

My bike lights stopped holding a charge this summer. They were still bright, the plastic was fine, and throwing them away felt silly, so I decided to try replacing the battery inside instead of buying a whole new pair of lights.

Here is what I learned doing it, in the order I did it, including the parts that went wrong and what I would do differently next time.

step 1: cut it open

The case is glued shut, so I ran a craft knife slowly around the seam until the two halves came apart. It took about ten minutes and some patience.

step 2: find the battery model

The cell is a small lithium polymer pouch with its model number printed on the side. I wrote it down and checked the voltage with a multimeter:

$ multimeter --dc
3.71 V

That matched the label, which meant the cell itself was the thing that had worn out rather than the charging circuit around it.

what I learned

Batteries are often the only part that wears out. Ordering a replacement cell and soldering two wires took far less time than I expected.

    def solder(wire_a, wire_b):
        return join(wire_a, wire_b)

Next time I will buy lights with a removable battery so none of this is needed.
